If you have shopped for an iPhone in Pakistan, you have seen three prices for what looks like the same phone: PTA approved, non-PTA, and a much cheaper "JV". This post explains what a JV phone is, why MobileUnder.pk never lists them, and why we think you should not buy or use one, even at a big discount.

## What "JV" means

Nobody agrees on what the letters stand for. The [OLX Pakistan blog](https://blog.olx.com.pk/mobiles/what-is-jv-iphone-is-it-worth-buying/) says "JV stands for 'joint venture'", [PhoneWorld](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/unregistered-jv-imei-tampered-mobile-phones-to-get-blocked-in-pakistan-soon/) calls them "joint venture/clone" devices, and [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) says shopkeepers use labels like "Joint Venture" or "Corporate Edition" to make them sound legitimate, and calls that "a fabrication".

What every source agrees on is the thing itself: **a JV phone is locked to a mobile network in another country**. [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) describes them as "Carrier-Locked devices smuggled from the US, Europe, or Japan", and [Bloom Pakistan](https://bloompakistan.com/jv-iphones-selling-across-pakistan-despite-pta-ban/) says they are typically phones bought on instalment plans abroad and then sent to Pakistan.

## How a JV phone is made to work here

A carrier-locked iPhone will not accept a Pakistani SIM. Only the original carrier can lift that lock: [Apple says](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT5014) "Apple can't unlock your iPhone for use with a different carrier. Only your current carrier can unlock your iPhone."

So sellers use workarounds. [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) describes a "Gevey" or "Turbo SIM" chip, "a thin circuit board placed underneath your actual SIM card" that "tricks the iPhone into thinking it's using the original carrier's network". [Daily Pakistan](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/14-Jun-2026/will-jv-iphones-in-pakistan-stop-working-after-ios-27-update-heres-what-we-know) calls these "carrier workarounds or SIM bypass solutions" and notes they are less stable than approved or factory unlocked phones. eSIM is not a way around it: [Ufone](https://www.ufone.com/esim/) states its eSIM "does not support carrier-locked handsets".

## JV, non-PTA and PTA approved: the difference

| | PTA approved | Non-PTA | JV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier lock | None | None (factory unlocked) | Locked to a foreign carrier |
| Works on a Pakistani SIM | Yes | Yes, for a limited time unless registered | Only through a chip or workaround |
| Can be registered with PTA | Already registered | Yes, by paying the tax | Consumer guides say generally not |
| Warranty | Official, if bought from an authorised seller | Usually seller only | None in practice |

On registration, [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) says "you cannot officially PTA approve these phones because their IMEIs are often blacklisted", and paying a tax would not remove a carrier lock anyway.

## Why MobileUnder.pk never lists JV phones

Our job is to help you buy a phone that works, keeps working, and comes with someone to stand behind it. A JV phone fails all three. We list PTA approved prices as the headline price, show non-PTA prices clearly labelled where they exist, and we never record a JV listing, however cheap.

## Why you should avoid using one

- **It can stop working without warning.** The chip can fail, and according to [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/), "if the chip fails, or if you update your iOS, the activation lock can return, killing your signal." Claims that a specific iOS update kills every JV phone are unverified; [Daily Pakistan](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/14-Jun-2026/will-jv-iphones-in-pakistan-stop-working-after-ios-27-update-heres-what-we-know) found "no verified evidence that all JV devices are being universally affected", but the risk is real and out of your control.
- **Its IMEI may already be blacklisted.** [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) says JV IMEIs are "often blacklisted by the original carrier abroad due to contract default or theft", and [Bloom Pakistan](https://bloompakistan.com/jv-iphones-selling-across-pakistan-despite-pta-ban/) explains that "if the original buyer stops payments or reports the device stolen, the phone is blacklisted." You cannot know which story your phone has.
- **PTA blocks non-compliant devices.** PTA's [DIRBS FAQ](https://dirbs.pta.gov.pk/faqs) lists replies for devices that will be blocked, including ones "Reported Stolen/Lost/Misused". PTA blocked roughly 100 million devices in FY2024-25, including about 27 million with duplicate or cloned IMEIs and around 868,000 lost or stolen handsets, as [The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1391342-pta-blocks-nearly-100m-mobile-devices) reported. [PhoneWorld](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/unregistered-jv-imei-tampered-mobile-phones-to-get-blocked-in-pakistan-soon/) adds that once a device is blocked, it cannot be restored to a local network without proper legal compliance.
- **"Fixed" JV phones are worse.** Some sellers offer to make a JV phone look approved by copying another phone's IMEI onto it. [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) calls this "a serious crime (data forgery)", and when duplication is detected, the phones sharing that IMEI can be permanently blocked. PTA's own [FAQ](https://dirbs.pta.gov.pk/faqs) says a device whose IMEI does not belong to it "will be subject to blocking and legal action."
- **There is no warranty.** Apple [may restrict warranty service](https://www.apple.com/legal/warranty/products/ios-warranty-document-us.html) to the country where the phone was originally sold, and excludes products "modified to alter functionality or capability without the written permission of Apple". A chipped phone is exactly that.
- **The saving is smaller than it looks.** Prices move, but as one dated example, [Bloom Pakistan](https://bloompakistan.com/jv-iphones-selling-across-pakistan-despite-pta-ban/) reported an iPhone 16 Pro Max 256GB in June 2025 at about Rs 470,000 PTA approved, up to Rs 350,000 non-PTA, and as low as Rs 235,000 JV. A phone that may be blocked, cannot be repaired under warranty and has little resale value is not a bargain at any of those gaps.

## How to check a phone before you pay

1. **Check the IMEI with PTA.** Dial `*#06#` to see the IMEI, then send it by SMS to 8484, or check it on the [DIRBS website](https://dirbs.pta.gov.pk/faqs). On a dual SIM phone, check both IMEIs.
2. **Make sure the reply matches the phone.** [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/guides/pta-approval-in-2026-how-to-dodge-the-imei-block-and-save-your-wallet/) notes that a genuine approval always matches the model name. If an iPhone comes back as a different model, its IMEI belongs to another device.
3. **Match the box.** PTA advises buying boxed phones so the IMEI on the handset can be matched to the box, and buying from reliable retailers under warranty, as [Aaj News](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330372004/pta-warns-mobile-phone-buyers-against-duplicate-devices) reported.
4. **Check the carrier lock on an iPhone.** Go to Settings, General, About. [Apple says](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT5014) that if "No SIM restrictions" appears next to Carrier Lock, the iPhone is unlocked. Anything else means it is locked.
5. **Look in the SIM tray.** The unlocking chip sits under the SIM card, so pull the tray and look for a thin circuit board.
6. **Walk away if the seller avoids any of this.** A real deal survives a two minute check.
